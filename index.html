How content on the new site will be organized

This is an explanation of the proposed content model, not a wireframe or prototype. Nothing on this page implies visual design, layout, or page templates.

Introduction

This page explains two things: how content will be organized in the back end of the new website (content types), and how that organization will surface for visitors as labels and filters (taxonomies).

We are sharing the information architecture earlier than we normally would because we want your feedback on these structures, especially the taxonomy terms, before we survey external users about them.

Content types

A content type is the shape a piece of content takes in the back end: which fields it has and where it lives. Every item on the site belongs to exactly one content type.

One of these is less intuitive than the rest at first glance. A food vendor, a public art piece, and a restroom do not seem to belong together. But each one is a physical place on the island that appears on the interactive map, and that is how people will experience them on the site. Because of the map, keeping these in one group feels intuitive to visitors on the front end, and it makes the content easier to manage as one consistent set on the back end. The labels below handle the differentiation between kinds of places.

Taxonomies

A taxonomy is a set of labels applied to content. Labels are what visitors actually see and use: filter chips, category names, badges. A single taxonomy can apply to several content types.

Some of these become filter chips on the interactive island map. Some appear in only one place on the site, and one may end up as a plain field rather than a set of labels. Open any taxonomy below to see where visitors encounter it and what is still unsettled. Feedback on those edges is exactly what we are looking for.
